# Art and licensing

Agent Pet renders sprite packs it did not make. This is what we do and do not do with them.

## The short version

**We render other people's pets. We never redistribute them.**

That is one rule, and it does not depend on whether the app is free.

## Where the packs come from

The pet atlas format originated in ChatGPT.app's own pet feature and is carried by community galleries — principally [Petdex](https://petdex.dev), which had 4 289 pets when we last looked. Agent Pet reads the directories those galleries install into (`~/.petdex/pets`, `~/.codex/pets`), so `npx petdex install <slug>` works for our users without us shipping a catalogue, a CLI, or a byte of anyone's art.

Those galleries are explicit about what they host: *"Pets are user-submitted fan art. AgentPets does not claim rights to any underlying IP"* / *"pet assets remain owned by their submitters and original rights holders."* A keyword pass over the manifest surfaces Homelander, Goku, Pikachu, Doraemon, Totoro, Naruto, Kirby, Gojo and Hello Kitty in the first sweep alone, and 2 132 of the pets are typed `character`, so the real count is far higher.

## What that means for us

**Rendering is not distributing.** A user installs a pack; the bytes travel from the gallery's CDN to their disk; we read the file and draw it. That is the same relationship a browser has with an image, or an image viewer with a JPEG.

**Committing it would be distributing.** The moment a pack lands in this repository or inside an installer, we become the party handing it out. So:

1. **No third-party art in this repository or in a build.** The bundled pet is generated by `tools/make-default-pack/generate.py` from flat colour and primitives — ours, reproducibly. A CC0 pack would also qualify.
2. **No re-hosting, mirroring, or caching to a CDN.** The gallery manifest is fetched lazily and only when the user opens the picker; downloads go from their machine to the gallery's servers.
3. **No character names anywhere we publish.** Not in screenshots, not in the README, not in a store listing. Every promotional frame uses our own pet.
4. **Never write to another tool's install directory.** We read `~/.petdex/pets` and `~/.codex/pets`. They belong to those tools.

This is enforced, not just intended: `.gitignore` excludes `*.webp` and `artifacts/**/*.png`, and the spike tooling that downloads real sheets writes to a scratch directory outside the repo.

## Why the rule survives the app being free

Being free changes the *risk*, not the *legality*. Fan art of a copyrighted character is unlicensed whether or not money changes hands; what free distribution usually buys you is a takedown notice rather than a lawsuit.

But two things are unaffected by price, and they are the actual reasons for the rule:

- **Being the distributor is a different position from being a renderer**, legally and practically. One receives takedown notices; the other has nothing to take down.
- **This repository is MIT-licensed.** We cannot place someone else's art under a licence we do not own it under. Committing a pack would make the LICENSE file a false statement about part of the tree.

Neither of those gets cheaper if the app is given away.

## If you author a pack

See [`PET_PACKS.md`](PET_PACKS.md). Draw something you own, or something you have the rights to share. If you submit it to a gallery, that gallery's terms apply — not ours.

## If you are a rights holder

Nothing in this repository contains third-party art, so there is nothing here to take down. Packs are hosted by the galleries listed above; their takedown processes apply. If you believe something in *this* repository infringes, open an issue and it will be removed.
